# Bath toys by age

The best bath toy is the one that buys you ten quiet minutes. Which toy that
is changes fast between three and nine, so here is what we have seen work.

## Three to five: squeeze, pour, squirt

At this age the fun is cause and effect. Squeeze the shark, water comes out.
Tip the cup, the wheel spins. Good picks:

- **Squirters and wind-up swimmers.** Soft, chunky, no small parts. Rinse and
  squeeze them dry after the bath or they go mouldy inside.
- **Foam letters and bath crayons.** Wet foam sticks to tiles, crayons wash
  off. Spelling practice sneaks in without anyone noticing.
- **A fishing net with floating fish.** Scooping and counting, over and over.

Skip anything with a battery compartment unless a grown-up handles it, and
skip anything small enough to fit in a mouth.

## Six to eight: build the waterfall

Now they want to make something. Suction-cup pipes, funnels and spinners
turn the tiled wall into a water run that is different every night. A
bubble machine on the tub wall adds drama. The tidy-up is a mesh bag hung
on the tap so things drain.

## Nine to twelve: the bath is a lab

Older kids are mostly done with bath toys, but not with water. Crystal
growing kits, pour-and-measure science sets and a cheap waterproof torch
for "deep sea" bath nights get a surprising amount of use. If the kit has
chemicals, a grown-up stays in the room, every time.

## The safety bits, briefly

- Never leave a child under about six alone in the bath, toys or no toys.
- Empty and air-dry anything that holds water. Mould grows inside squirters
  within a week if they stay wet.
- Check the age grade on each product page. We put it next to the price so
  you do not have to hunt.
