# How to read a toy age grade

Every toy at Bubble Sharks shows an age grade near the price. It is the most
useful number on the page, and the most misread one. Here is what it means.

## It is a safety line before it is a skill level

In the US, toy makers test against the ASTM F963 standard and label the
result. "3+" means the toy passed the tests for children aged three and up,
and it usually means there are small parts inside. Small parts are the
reason "3+" exists: a child under three puts things in their mouth, and a
piece smaller than a film canister can be swallowed or inhaled.

So the first rule is simple. If the youngest child in the house is under
three, treat "3+" as "not yet", even if the toy is meant for an older
sibling. We do not list toys with small parts, magnets, button batteries or
long cords for under-threes at all.

## Then it is a skill guide

Above three, the grade tells you what the toy expects from a child:

- **3–5:** chunky pieces, no reading, quick wins. Bath squirters, foam
  letters, stacking games, big floor puzzles.
- **6–8:** following a picture guide, taking turns, building something that
  takes twenty minutes. Marble runs, gear sets, family games.
- **9–12:** reading instructions, patience, a little science. Solar robot
  kits, crystal growing, a real microscope.

A toy graded below a child's age is fine; it just gets outgrown sooner. A
toy graded above it tends to end up in the cupboard, or in a parent's hands.

## What we check before a toy goes in the shop

- The supplier states the age grade and can show the US test report.
- No small parts, magnets or button batteries in anything graded for
  under-threes.
- The "Age & safety" tab on the product page lists the grade, the
  materials and a supervision note. If a toy needs a grown-up in the room,
  it says so.

If you are not sure which grade fits a particular kid, email
support@bubblesharks.com with their age and what they like. A real person
answers within a business day.
