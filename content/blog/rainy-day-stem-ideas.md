# Rainy-day STEM: five builds that keep a seven-year-old busy

"STEM toy" is a label that gets stuck on anything with a gear in it. The
ones that actually hold a child's attention have one thing in common: the
child decides what to build, and it works or it does not. Five ideas, from
free to about forty dollars.

## 1. The cardboard marble run (free)

Tape kitchen-roll tubes to a wall at angles and roll a marble through. It
fails constantly, which is the point. When they ask for "a real one", an
80-piece marble run set takes the same idea and lets them rebuild it every
day without tape.

## 2. The gear chain

A gears building set is a puzzle in disguise. Turn one gear, make the one
across the board spin. It takes a few tries to work out that the chain has
to touch all the way along. Expect a few "it doesn't work" moments before
the big grin.

## 3. Magnetic tiles, rules edition

Tiles are great free play, but a small rule makes them a challenge: build
the tallest tower using exactly twenty tiles, or a garage the toy car fits
in. Ten minutes of planning, twenty of building, then it gets knocked over,
which is also fine.

## 4. Solar robot, window edition

A 12-in-1 solar robot kit is a 9+ toy on the box, but a seven-year-old can
build the simpler models with a grown-up reading the steps. It moves when
you put it on a sunny windowsill and stops when you shade it with your
hand. Children find this genuinely hilarious.

## 5. The take-apart truck (younger siblings welcome)

If a four-year-old needs including, a take-apart truck with a toy drill
keeps both busy: the little one drills the bolts out, the big one puts it
back together faster.
